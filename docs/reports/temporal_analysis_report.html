Structural connectomics · Study 129 · Analysis report

Structural network trajectories across a two- or four-week solo versus group running intervention

Whole-brain diffusion connectomes, AAL3 parcellation, three timepoints.
125 participants · 339 scans · factorial mixed-effects and latent-growth analysis
bctpy_mrtrix / 7_temporal_analysis · compiled 11 September 2026

Abstract

Background. Aerobic exercise has been reported to shift structural brain networks toward greater integration, but most evidence comes from longer interventions in older or clinical samples. Whether a two- to four-week running programme measurably reorganises white-matter networks in healthy young adults — and whether the social context of that exercise matters — is unresolved.

Methods. 125 young adults (18–38 y) were allocated to a no-running control arm or to one of four running arms in a 2 × 2 factorial of social context (running alone vs. running in a group) and duration (two vs. four weeks). Diffusion MRI was acquired at three timepoints. Whole-brain connectomes were reconstructed with GQI tractography and parcellated with AAL3. Graph-theoretical measures were reduced by principal component analysis after their redundancy was quantified, and analysed with linear mixed-effects models using four orthogonal planned contrasts crossed with session. Tractography yield, edge-set instability, and multiplicity were addressed by explicit sensitivity analyses.

1 Introduction

Physical exercise is among the few behavioural interventions with a reproducible effect on brain structure. Most of that literature concerns grey-matter volume or cerebral perfusion; the evidence that exercise reorganises the white-matter network is thinner, and is drawn largely from interventions lasting months rather than weeks, in older or clinical cohorts where baseline decline leaves more room for change.

Two questions motivate the present analysis. First, whether a short intervention — two to four weeks of running — produces any detectable change in the topology of the structural connectome of healthy young adults, a population in which large change is not expected a priori. Second, and more specifically, whether the social context in which the exercise is performed modifies that change. Running in a group differs from running alone in social contact, in coordination demands, and in adherence; these are candidate modifiers that a purely dose-based account of exercise effects would not predict.

The design allows the second question to be asked cleanly. Comparing any running arm against a control arm that did nothing confounds exercise with everything else that differs between participating in a structured programme and not doing so. Comparing group running against solo running does not: both arms ran, for the same prescribed durations, and differ only in the social manipulation. That contrast is therefore the internally controlled one, and is treated here as the contrast of primary interest.

A third, methodological aim is addressed alongside. Graph-theoretical connectome analyses routinely report a battery of a dozen or more "measures" and treat convergent significance across them as corroboration. This analysis quantifies how much independent information such a battery actually carries, and how much of the leading measure — network density — is attributable to tractography yield rather than to anatomy.

2 Methods

2.1 Participants and design

Of 177 enrolled participants, 125 contributed usable diffusion data and a study-arm assignment and form the analysed sample (339 scan sessions). One further participant had connectome data but no recorded arm allocation and was excluded. Participants were young adults aged 18–38 years (mean 24.3, SD 4.7).

Allocation followed a 2 × 2 factorial of social context (running alone; running in a group) and duration (two weeks; four weeks), with an additional no-running control arm (Table 1). Participants in the two-week arms had completed the running programme approximately two weeks before the final scan; participants in the four-week arms ran until shortly before it. Three diffusion scans were acquired per participant (ses-1, ses-2, ses-3). Scan dates were not available to this analysis, so equal inter-scan intervals were assumed throughout.

2.2 Diffusion MRI and tractography

Connectomes were reconstructed with the laboratory's DSI Studio pipeline (MRI-Lab-Graz). Orientation distribution functions were estimated by generalised q-sampling imaging (GQI). Whole-brain deterministic tractography generated 5 000 000 streamlines per scan, restricted to streamline lengths between 30 and 250 mm. Connectivity matrices were assembled by "pass"-type endpoint counting with a relative connectivity threshold of 0.001, i.e. entries below one thousandth of the largest entry were set to zero.

2.3 Network construction

Nodes were the 166 regions of the AAL3 atlas. Analyses were restricted to this single parcellation. An earlier exploratory pass had examined five atlases in parallel; because all five are derived from the same subjects and the same streamline sets, agreement between them indexes parcellation robustness but does not constitute independent replication, and reporting it as such overstates the evidence. AAL3 was retained as the single a priori parcellation.

Edges were weighted by streamline count normalised by the volumes of the two connected regions (ROI normalisation), which prevents large regions from appearing artefactually over-connected. Two alternatives were considered and rejected: raw streamline counts, whose scale is incompatible with the weighted clustering coefficient and yields uninterpretable magnitudes; and division by the single largest edge, which compresses weighted clustering and local efficiency to implausibly small values because the maximum is frequently an outlier.

One AAL3 region (0-indexed node 128; 8 mm³ in the atlas volume table, consistent with a small cerebellar-vermis subdivision) had no streamlines to any other region in 100% of scans, rendering characteristic path length infinite. Because the 30 mm minimum-length filter removes essentially all connections of a region a few voxels across, this reflects the interaction of parcellation granularity with tractography settings rather than anatomy. The region was excluded before computing any measure, leaving 165 nodes. No other AAL3 region exceeded a 25% isolation rate.

2.4 Graph-theoretical measures

Eleven whole-brain measures were computed per scan with the Brain Connectivity Toolbox (bctpy): density, characteristic path length, global efficiency, modularity, core–periphery structure, mean strength, mean local efficiency, mean clustering coefficient, mean betweenness centrality, mean subgraph centrality, and small-worldness. Weighted measures used the ROI-normalised matrix; path length, global efficiency and subgraph centrality were computed on the binarised topology, following the convention of the reference pipeline this analysis adapts.

Three implementation choices differ from that reference pipeline and are stated for reproducibility. Betweenness centrality was computed on a connection-length matrix obtained by inverting non-zero weights, as the toolbox specifies; passing a weight matrix directly, as the reference implementation does, inverts the meaning of the shortest path. Modularity was estimated with the Louvain algorithm (weighted, fixed seed) because the toolbox's Newman implementation fails under the installed NumPy version. Subgraph centrality, which grows exponentially with the largest eigenvalue and spans ten orders of magnitude across scans, was log₁₀-transformed. Two further measures from the reference battery — k-core and s-core size — were dropped because their fixed thresholds were calibrated for a different weight scale and saturate on these denser connectomes.

Small-worldness was computed as σ = (C/Crand)/(L/Lrand) against 100 degree-preserving randomised null networks per scan.

2.5 Redundancy and dimension reduction

Before modelling, the covariance structure of the eleven measures was examined. Measure batteries of this kind are widely reported as though their elements were separate outcomes; when they are strongly intercorrelated, convergent significance across them is not independent corroboration but the same result restated. The effective number of independent dimensions was estimated as (Σλ)² ⁄ Σλ², where λ are the eigenvalues of the measure correlation matrix.

The battery was then reduced by principal component analysis on z-scored measures across all complete scans. The first three components were retained as the primary outcomes, which fixes the primary test family at three components × four planned contrasts = twelve tests rather than the fifty-odd that testing every measure against every arm would produce.

2.6 Statistical analysis

The five arms form a factorial design, and were analysed with four orthogonal planned contrasts rather than four arbitrary comparisons against control:

Each measure was modelled with a linear mixed-effects model with a by-participant random intercept:

outcome ~ (running + context + duration + context×duration) × session + age + sex + (1 | participant)

Models were fitted with lme4/lmerTest using Satterthwaite degrees of freedom, and Type III F tests were taken for each term. The contrast × session terms carry the intervention effects: an arm difference that is constant across sessions is a pre-existing group difference, whereas a difference that develops across sessions is what an intervention effect looks like. Direction and magnitude were read from a companion model in which session entered as a linear score (0, 1, 2), so that each contrast × time coefficient is a difference in slope per session, reported in units of the outcome's standard deviation.

Within the primary family of twelve tests, p-values were corrected by the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure.

2.7 Sensitivity analyses

Four sensitivity analyses were pre-specified, each targeting a specific way in which the primary result could be an artefact.

Tractography yield. Network density is the proportion of node pairs joined by a surviving edge, and therefore depends on how many streamlines the tractography returned and on where the relative threshold fell. A group difference in yield would present as a group difference in density. Every model was refitted with the scan's total raw streamline count as an additional covariate.

Fixed edge set. To remove yield as a mechanism entirely, a consistency-thresholded backbone was constructed comprising the edges present in at least 75% of all scans. This edge set is identical for every participant and session, so density is constant by construction and only edge weights can vary. Measures recomputed on the backbone isolate change in connection strength from change in which connections were detected.

Permutation inference. With 15–41 participants per arm, the asymptotic reference distribution of the F statistic is not guaranteed. An exact reference distribution was built by permuting arm labels between participants — preserving each participant's within-subject trajectory intact — and refitting the primary models 2000 times.

Baseline dependence. To distinguish an intervention effect from regression toward the mean in an arm that happened to start low, models were also refitted as an analysis of covariance on the post-baseline sessions with each participant's baseline value as a covariate.

3 Results

3.1 Network construction and data quality

All 339 scans yielded a connectome. After exclusion of the isolated region, 5.3% of scans still returned an undefined characteristic path length owing to a sporadically disconnected node, and were dropped listwise from the measures affected; density, strength and clustering are defined regardless and retained the full sample.

Density correlated r = 0.61 with the total raw streamline count of the same scan and r = 0.70 with mean ROI-normalised strength; that is, roughly 37% of the between-scan variance in density is shared with a purely methodological quantity. Density was essentially uncorrelated with the single largest raw edge (r = −0.05), confirming that the dependency runs through overall yield rather than through the relative threshold's anchor. This motivated the yield covariate and the fixed-edge-set backbone described in §2.7.

Edge presence was highly variable across scans. Only 130 of the 13 530 possible connections (1.0%) were present in every scan, whereas 2461 (18.2%) were present in at least half. The backbone used for sensitivity analysis — edges present in at least 75% of scans — comprised 1629 edges (12.0% of possible).

3.2 The measure battery carries about three dimensions

The eleven whole-brain measures were far from independent (Figure 2). Density, global efficiency, path length and subgraph centrality formed a single tightly coupled cluster (|r| = 0.94–0.99 among all pairs); mean clustering coefficient and mean local efficiency were perfectly collinear (r = 1.00); and small-worldness correlated r = 0.95 with both.

The effective number of independent dimensions was 2.95 out of a nominal eleven. Reporting convergent effects on density, global efficiency, path length and subgraph centrality as four mutually supporting findings therefore restates one finding four times. The three retained principal components are interpreted in §3.3 and serve as the primary outcomes.

3.3 Component structure

The three retained components accounted for 88.6% of the variance in the battery (47.6%, 31.1% and 9.9%) and separated cleanly into interpretable axes (Table 4). Component signs, which are arbitrary in principal component analysis, were oriented so that higher scores denote more of the named property.

PC1 (integration, 47.6%) contrasts density, global efficiency and subgraph centrality against characteristic path length and modularity: a densely connected, short-path, weakly modular network scores high. PC2 (segregation, 31.1%) is defined almost entirely by clustering coefficient, local efficiency and small-worldness, which load on it at 0.52–0.53 and essentially nowhere else. PC3 (centralisation, 9.9%) carries betweenness centrality and core–periphery structure against modularity — networks whose traffic concentrates on a small core.

The decomposition makes the redundancy concrete. The four measures on which the exploratory analysis had reported convergent effects — density, global efficiency, subgraph centrality and path length — are the four highest-loading measures of a single component, with no appreciable loading on either of the others. They were never four findings.
